# Process-improvement recommendations

## Current-state hypothesis

Requests enter through multiple channels, are manually routed, and may not have a consistent category or urgency definition. That creates avoidable delay and makes it hard to compare performance across buildings or teams.

## Future-state workflow

1. **Capture:** Require category, subcategory, location, and priority at intake.
2. **Triage:** Send emergency and high-priority items to a monitored queue.
3. **Assign:** Route by category and current team workload.
4. **Escalate:** Alert a supervisor when the assignment timer is approaching its SLA.
5. **Resolve:** Require a resolution note and close-out timestamp.
6. **Learn:** Review weekly trends, SLA misses, reopens, and recurring issue types.

## Requirements

- The intake form should prevent blank category and priority values.
- The system should flag duplicate requests from the same hall and issue type within a short time window.
- Managers should be able to filter requests by hall, category, priority, status, and assigned team.
- The scorecard should show request volume, open backlog, assignment time, resolution time, SLA rate, and reopen rate.
- The dashboard should distinguish measured results from projections or targets.

## Measurement plan

Compare a four-week baseline with four weeks after the workflow change. Track average and median assignment time, high-priority SLA rate, open backlog, reopen rate, and satisfaction. Segment results by hall and category so an overall average does not hide a local bottleneck.
